# Cluster B: Agentic RL and Preference Optimization for SWE Agents (2024-Oct 2026)

Scope: which RL / preference / distillation recipes are feasible and highest-value for LoRA post-training of Gemma 4 E4B/12B/31B (QAT int4, vLLM) on Kaggle L4x4 (4x24 GB, no NVLink, ~15 GPU-h/week/account, 2-3 accounts, no internet in GPU sessions, 12 h sessions), with execution-based rewards from 129 public tasks (+ synthetic), a fixed 9-tool harness, and a ~6 min/task eval budget.

Note on sourcing: the arXiv MCP server timed out on most calls, so abstracts were pulled from arxiv.org/abs pages and HTML full texts via fetch. All IDs below were verified against the arXiv page except where marked.

## 1. Method table

### Takeaway
Every published SWE-agent RL result that moves SWE-bench Verified by >10 points used 64-128 H100/H200-class GPUs or 8 B200s and 100-340 policy updates with 8-10 rollouts per task on thousands of tasks. The only 4B recipe at a budget we can approach (SWE-Ladder: 70 iterations x 64 rollouts = 4,480 trajectories) got its gain from privileged self-distillation, not from GRPO, which was nearly flat (+0.8 pp) at that budget.

### Inferences
- The 4B-class evidence splits: Arctic-SWE reaches 62-65% with hundreds of async updates on B200s; SWE-Ladder with ~4.5K total rollouts gets +0.8 pp from GRPO and +6.8 pp only by adding privileged self-distillation. Our budget is closer to SWE-Ladder's than to anyone else's.

## 2. Cheap alternatives to online RL

### Takeaway
Rejection-sampling FT roughly doubles a weak base's pass@1 in one round (11.4 -> 20.5 at 72B); on-policy distillation reproduces or beats RL at ~1/10 the GPU hours when an open teacher is available; privileged self-distillation needs no bigger teacher at all and is the only method with a 4B SWE result at a few-thousand-rollout budget.

### Cited Findings
- RFT: Nebius sampled 10 trajectories per task on filtered SWE-rebench tasks, kept only those passing tests (6,548 trajectories), masked turns with formatting errors, 1 SFT epoch (lr 5e-6, batch 64, 50 steps, 65k ctx): 11.4% -> 20.5% Pass@1; one round only — [2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2).
- SWE-Gym: one round of rejection-sampling self-improvement reached 19.7% (32B) / 10.0% (7B) on Lite; "performance scales with the number of sampled trajectories up to our current limit of 491 trajectories"; sampling compute, not task count, was the bottleneck — [2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139).
- Skywork-SWE: SFT on >8,000 validated teacher trajectories "continues to improve as the data size increases, showing no signs of saturation"; 38.0% Pass@1 at 32B — [2506.19290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19290). SWE-smith distills Claude 3.7/GPT-4o trajectories into a 32B student (40.2% per a secondary source) — [2504.21798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798).
- On-policy distillation (Qwen3 Table 21, Qwen3-8B): off-policy distill 55.0 AIME'24 -> +RL 67.6 at 17,920 GPU-h vs +on-policy distill 74.4 at 1,800 GPU-h; teachers Qwen3-32B or 235B-A22B; student logits aligned to teacher by KL; students 0.6B-14B and 30B-A3B — [2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1).
- Thinking Machines: per-token reverse KL on student samples as the advantage, discount 0, importance-sampling loss; "teacher log-probs require only one forward pass, and partial rollouts can be used"; ~70% AIME'24 in ~150 steps (64 prompts x 4 samples); 9x FLOP / ~18x GPU-hour reduction vs extrapolated SFT, ~30x including teacher data-generation cost; self-distillation from an RL-trained same-base teacher matched it in 7-10x fewer steps; LoRA rank 32 trailed full FT by 13% after SFT but 6% after on-policy distillation; rank 128 used in the RL comparison. The post does not state a tokenizer constraint, but the method scores the student's sampled token IDs, which implies a shared vocabulary — [thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/).
- Privileged self-distillation for SWE: SWE-Ladder's frozen same-base teacher conditioned on stage-specific gold evidence gives 16.4% vs 10.4% GRPO at 4B with 4,480 rollouts; full-patch OPSD alone 12.4%; stage order matters (reversed 11.4%); loss is a stop-gradient top-16 surrogate, lambda 0.15 — [2610.33667](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33667v1). General framework: pi-Distill, RL with reverse-KL to a privileged-information teacher — [2602.04942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04942); critiques of OPSD "privilege illusion" — [2608.25936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25936), [2608.01735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01735).
- Multi-teacher OPD to merge category experts, no external model: +5.39 pp Pro-618 — [2609.23377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23377).
- Offline step-level DPO: Agentic-DPO turns expert trajectories into state-conditioned preferences (expert action vs plausible wrong one-step samples); 9B tau-bench 21.7% (SFT) -> 41.4%, "matching online GRPO under the same backbone" with no environment interaction during training — [2607.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601). Older: Agent Q (MCTS + off-policy DPO, Llama-3 70B WebShop/booking 18.6 -> 81.7) — [2408.07199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07199); IPR (MC step rewards along the expert trajectory, contrastive action pairs) — [2406.11176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11176). On a 2B search agent, synthetic step-DPO added only ~1 pt over trajectory SFT — [2608.29357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29357).

### Inferences
- With 129 tasks, one RFT round needs ~10-16 samples/task (1,300-2,000 trajectories) to harvest enough passes; at low base pass rates the yield is the binding constraint, so synthetic tasks matter more for RFT than for RL.
- Gemma 4 31B as OPD teacher for E4B is feasible in principle if the family shares a tokenizer (expected for Gemma, unverified); the teacher pass is prefill-only over student trajectories, so it costs far less than generation.
- SWE-Ladder-style privileged self-distillation maps directly onto our data: the 129 tasks' gold diffs and F2P tests are the privileged evidence; the frozen teacher is the same E4B.

### Gaps
- No SWE-agent study of RFT across multiple rounds; ReST-EM ([2312.06585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06585)) reports diminishing returns after a few iterations on math/code from my recollection, not re-verified here.
- No published on-policy distillation result on SWE-bench with a large open teacher; the closest are self-distillation variants.

## 3. Reward and credit-assignment design

### Takeaway
Binary hidden-test reward with hard gates (format/duplicate/budget -> 0) and a step-count penalty is the consensus; partial credit as fraction of target tests with a zero override on any P2P regression worked at frontier scale; trajectory-level advantages with sequence-level importance ratios are sufficient and more stable than token-level, while step-level credit methods that need extra rollouts or forkable environments are not affordable here.

### Cited Findings
- Binary, gated: DeepSWE 1 iff sampled P2P+F2P pass within 5 min, no partial credit ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/agentica-org/DeepSWE-Preview)); Arctic-SWE ends rollouts with 0 on duplicate tool calls, invalid format, exhausted turn/token budget, validates reasoning block / tool-call envelope / tags in raw output because the parser silently repairs, and uses -1 for invalid in the curated run — [2610.24914](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24914v1).
- Length: Nebius penalizes steps linearly beyond L_thr=10 up to T_max, not tokens; warns that discarding overflow trajectories "removes negative examples of looping behavior" — [2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2). DeepSWE instead masks truncated/timeout trajectories (compact filtering).
- Partial credit: Kimi K2.7 reward = fraction of target checks passed, 0 if any pass-to-pass test fails; trajectories got 24-35% shorter — [2610.00890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00890).
- Execution-free: SWE-RL patch-similarity reward (70B) — [2502.18449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18449).
- Reward refinement from existing rollouts: BSR has the policy pairwise-compare same-outcome trajectories' debugging behavior into bounded auxiliary rewards preserving pass/fail order; gains at Qwen3-14B/32B — [2610.12598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12598).
- Process rewards: SWE-MILE (file-exposure and test-state potentials, shadow sandbox probing) — [2609.32631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32631); CRR counterfactual forks: 41.7% vs 36.7% for equal-wall-clock GRPO at 14B, requires cheap state restoration — [2609.33875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33875); LVRL frozen LLM value function: up to +8.6 pp over GRPO on Verified, 4B-32B — [2610.15840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15840).
- Credit assignment: GiGPO groups actions by repeated env states ([2505.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10978)); ARPO branches at high-entropy post-tool steps ([2507.19849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19849)); CrEST anchors advantage sign to the verifier, magnitude to a privileged self-teacher ([2608.13179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13179)); ReSum-GRPO broadcasts the final advantage across summary-segmented trajectories ([2509.13313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13313)); CANOPY argues outcome-only suffices if exploration is scaled, updates are on-policy and KL-anchored, and loss is confined to action tokens ([2609.01245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01245)).
- Algorithm variants: GSPO sequence-level ratio with 1/|y| normalization, tolerant of engine mismatch in multi-turn/partial rollouts — [2507.18071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18071); ARLArena finds sequence-level clipping "a key factor for stabilizing multi-turn agentic RL" and proposes SAMPO — [2602.21534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21534). TRL exposes `loss_type` in {grpo, dapo (default), dr_grpo, sapo, cispo, vespo}, `importance_sampling_level="sequence"` (GSPO), `epsilon_high` (DAPO 0.28), `scale_rewards` in {group, batch, False}, `beta=0.0` default (no ref model), `mask_truncated_completions` — [TRL GRPO docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/grpo_trainer).
- Context compaction training: AutoCompact (judge-corrected compaction decisions -> SFT -> RL with task reward) +9.2 pp Verified, works at 16K window with fallback compaction — [2610.02163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02163); Context-Folding/FoldGRPO 10x smaller active context on SWE — [2510.11967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967); memory-tool RL — [2609.34422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34422); token-efficiency RL (HERO) — [2609.38885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38885).

### Inferences (recommendations)
- Reward = gated binary on hidden tests (0 for malformed tool JSON, duplicate calls, no submission, time/turn budget exhaustion), plus fraction-of-F2P partial credit with zero override on any P2P failure, plus a step penalty tuned to the 6-min eval budget. Our harness strips test edits, so the main hacking vector is closed; keep P2P as the regression gate.
- Use trajectory-level advantages with `importance_sampling_level="sequence"`, `beta=0`, no std scaling (Dr.GRPO), clip-high 0.28, loss on assistant tokens only. Do not mask truncated trajectories outright; give them the length penalty so looping is negatively reinforced.
- Skip step-level credit that needs extra rollouts (ARPO, CRR; GiGPO anchors rarely repeat in SWE). If dense signal is wanted, use the privileged-teacher KL (SWE-Ladder/CrEST), which needs no extra environment interaction.
- Train on compacted/truncated contexts explicitly: include trajectories where the prefix is replaced by a summary (ReSum-style advantage broadcasting) so the policy is robust to the harness's compaction.

### Gaps
- No controlled SWE comparison of GRPO vs DAPO vs GSPO vs Dr.GRPO vs REINFORCE++ at fixed budget; evidence is per-paper recipes.

## 4. LoRA-RL feasibility on 4x L4

### Takeaway
LoRA is not the bottleneck (rank 1-32 matches full FT in RL; Kimi K2.7 used rank 32 GSPO); rollout generation is. By first-principles estimates an E4B policy yields roughly 100-200 trajectories per wall-hour on L4x4, so one RL update (16 tasks x 8 rollouts) costs ~1 h and a SWE-Ladder-sized run (~4.5K rollouts) is ~25-40 h, i.e. one to two weeks of the combined team quota. 31B online RL is out of reach.

### Cited Findings
- LoRA Without Regret: with policy gradient "LoRA matched FullFT's learning even with ranks as low as 1" (Llama-3.1-8B GSM8K/MATH; Qwen3-8B-base DeepMath 8k); optimal LoRA LR ~10x full FT (fitted 9.8x), ~15x for runs <100 steps; LR nearly rank-independent; apply to MLP/MoE layers (attention-only underperforms); keep effective batch <32; RL absorbs ~O(1) bit/episode so a 3M-param rank-1 adapter exceeds the information in 320K episodes; LoRA uses ~2/3 of full-FT FLOPs — [thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/).
- Unsloth vLLM standby: shares weights between vLLM and trainer, KV cache and training share one pool; Qwen3-4B on a 16 GB T4 fits ~32K-token sequences with `gpu_memory_utilization=0.95` (OOM above ~2K without standby); claims TRL+LoRA handled only ~1K context for 8B; multi-GPU and agentic/tool-calling GRPO not covered — [Unsloth docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/memory-efficient-rl). Multi-GPU status is contested across third-party sources (Pro-tier vs open) with no primary confirmation — [example](https://dev.to/dibi8/unsloth-2026-649k-star-fast-llm-fine-tuning-302d).
- TRL: colocate mode (default) runs vLLM inside the trainer sharing GPU memory (`vllm_gpu_memory_utilization` default 0.3); server mode needs separate CUDA devices and `--weight-transfer-config '{"backend": "nccl"}'` (or `ipc` when sharing a GPU), `--logprobs-mode processed_logprobs`, `--max-logprobs -1` for teacher distributions; supports vLLM 0.22.0-0.32.x; `vllm_enable_sleep_mode` offloads during the optimizer step; `environment_factory` (reset/get_reward, requires transformers>=5.2.0, experimental), `rollout_func`, `tools`, `max_tool_calling_iterations`; prefix-preserving chat template required; a GRPO+QLoRA Colab notebook exists but the GRPO agent notebook is flagged OOM on Colab GPUs — [vLLM integration](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/vllm_integration), [GRPO trainer](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/grpo_trainer), [TRL examples](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/v1.7.0/example_overview).
- Agent frameworks: SkyRL-Agent interoperates with SkyRL-train, verl, and Tinker backends — [2511.16108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108); Agent Lightning decouples agent runtime from trainer — [2508.03680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03680); Arctic-SWE and Nebius run rollouts as Kubernetes pods / Docker images per task.
- Sampling bias: Nebius found top_k/min_p in vLLM degraded training after 5-10 iterations — [2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2). Arctic-SWE's rollout-trainer mismatch KL ~1e-4 needed FP32 recurrent state; CISPO bounded IS ratios mitigate — [2610.24914](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24914v1).
- Harness choice: performance varies 7.13 pp across harnesses, more than RL gains — [2609.04518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04518).

### Inferences (throughput estimate; no L4x4 GRPO benchmark exists, so this is derived)
- Assumptions: L4 ~300 GB/s, ~121 dense bf16 TFLOPS, 24 GB (NVIDIA spec sheet, from memory). Decode is bandwidth-bound: E4B bf16 weights ~8 GB -> ~35 forward passes/s ceiling per GPU; int4 QAT weights (~2.5-3 GB) raise the ceiling but LoRA-on-quantized-base in vLLM must be validated. With ~8 concurrent sequences per GPU, expect ~20-35 tok/s/stream, 150-300 tok/s aggregate per GPU at 32k context (KV cache a few GB).
- A SWE trajectory of ~30 turns generates ~6-10k tokens (tool output is prefill): ~5-7 min generation plus test execution. Three rollout GPUs x 8 concurrent -> ~100-200 trajectories/hour; the fourth GPU trains the LoRA (E4B bf16 base + rank-32 adapter + 32k activations with gradient checkpointing fits in 24 GB; 12B does not without the base in 4-bit and sharding, which has no NVLink).
- One update of 16 tasks x 8 rollouts = 128 trajectories ~ 45-75 min. Weekly budget (~15 h/account, 2-3 accounts) -> ~30-50 updates/week team-wide. SWE-Ladder's 70 x 64 = 4,480 rollouts ~ 25-40 h; Arctic-SWE's 340 updates x 128 rollouts is ~300+ h and out of reach.
- On-policy distillation from Gemma 4 31B int4 (TP=4 across all four L4s) must be phased within a session: generate student rollouts -> unload -> score teacher logprobs (prefill-only, tens of seconds per 32k trajectory) -> unload -> train. 31B cannot be co-resident with student vLLM and trainer in 96 GB.
- Tooling without Docker: TRL `environment_factory`/`rollout_func` with the harness run in-process (subprocess per task in the notebook container) is the only path that does not require Ray/K8s; verl, rLLM, SkyRL are Ray-based with Docker-per-task sandboxes and pinned vLLM versions and should be treated as infeasible on Kaggle. All wheels and checkpoints must be pre-staged as Kaggle datasets.

### Gaps
- Whether TRL merges LoRA weights before NCCL/IPC sync to vLLM (vs vLLM LoRA hot-swap) was not stated in the fetched docs; needs a code check.
- No measured L4x4 numbers for Gemma 4 E4B under vLLM; the estimate above should be benchmarked in the first session.
- Gemma 4 QAT int4 + LoRA + vLLM compatibility not verified in this cluster.

## 5. Most promising ideas for us (ranked by expected gain per GPU-hour)

1. RFT / expert iteration in our exact harness (1-2 rounds): sample 8-16 trajectories per task on the 129 + synthetic tasks, keep test-passing ones, mask malformed turns, 1 epoch LoRA SFT. Evidence: 11.4 -> 20.5 at 72B in one round ([2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03501)); SWE-Gym scaling with sampled trajectories ([2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139)). Cheapest; uses only inference compute.
2. Privileged on-policy self-distillation (SWE-Ladder): frozen E4B teacher sees gold files/lines, F2P tests, and diff per stage; L_GRPO + 0.15 L_OPSD; staged LOC -> REP -> PATCH. Only 4B SWE result at ~4.5K rollouts; +6.8 pp vs +0.8 for GRPO ([2610.33667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33667)). Needs no bigger model.
3. On-policy distillation from Gemma 4 31B to E4B LoRA (phased teacher scoring): 1/10 the GPU hours of RL at equal or better results in Qwen3 ([2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)); reverse-KL per token, partial rollouts allowed ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). Requires shared tokenizer (expected) and validated int4-31B vLLM logprobs.
4. Agentic-DPO offline from teacher/self trajectories: state-conditioned expert-vs-wrong-action pairs, no environment rollouts at train time; 9B 21.7 -> 41.4 tau-bench matching online GRPO ([2607.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)). Pairs well with the <$50 API teacher budget (a few hundred teacher trajectories).
5. Compaction-robust training: inject summary-compacted prefixes into SFT/RFT data and broadcast outcome advantage across segments; AutoCompact +9.2 pp, ReSum-GRPO +8.2 ([2610.02163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02163), [2509.13313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13313)).
6. Short GRPO/CISPO polish run (20-40 updates, rank 32, gated binary + P2P-zeroed partial credit + step penalty, sequence-level IS, beta=0) only after 1-4: Kimi K2.7 one-epoch rank-32 GSPO shortened trajectories 24-35% ([2610.00890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00890)); expect low single-digit pp, main value is turn efficiency under the 6-min budget ([2609.38885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38885)).
7. Failure-conditioned recovery data (FC-SWE): after a failed attempt, re-run with the failed patch + test output in context; use the recovery trajectories for RFT/OPSD (41.7 vs 38.9 R@1 at 4B) ([2610.07898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07898)).
8. Behavioral self-rewarding to break ties among same-outcome RFT candidates (choose which passing trajectory to keep) without new rollouts ([2610.12598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12598)).

## 6. Not feasible and why

- Online RL on Gemma 4 31B (or 12B without aggressive offload): 31B int4 needs all four L4s for vLLM TP alone; no room for a trainer, and no NVLink for sharded backward over 32k contexts. 12B is marginal at best.
- DeepSWE / Arctic-SWE / Nebius-scale runs: 100-340 updates x 128-1,280 rollouts on 64-128 H100/H200 or 8 B200 ([2610.24914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24914), [2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03501)); two orders of magnitude above ~15 h/week/account.
- verl / rLLM / SkyRL / Agent Lightning on Kaggle: Ray clusters, Docker-per-task sandboxes, pinned vLLM builds, no internet at runtime. SkyRL's Tinker backend is a hosted service outside the API budget.
- Step-level credit that multiplies rollouts: ARPO branching, CRR counterfactual forks (fork overhead), SWE-MILE shadow probing, LVRL value-LLM calls ([2507.19849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19849), [2609.33875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33875), [2609.32631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32631), [2610.15840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15840)). GiGPO's anchor states rarely repeat in repo editing.
- Unsloth multi-GPU GRPO: status unconfirmed in primary sources; single-GPU standby mode is fine for E4B SFT/RFT but agentic multi-turn GRPO is undocumented.
- SWE-RL patch-similarity reward as the main signal: designed for single-shot Agentless patching at 70B; our hidden tests give a better signal, though similarity could score synthetic tasks lacking tests.
