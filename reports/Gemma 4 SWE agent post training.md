# Harness-exact SFT beats RL under Kaggle limits

The 2024–Oct 2026 literature and the Kaggle community record point to one dominant recipe for our constraints: take the tens of thousands of permissively licensed SWE-agent trajectories already on Hugging Face, re-map them deterministically into the competition's 9-tool Gemma 4 format, filter them to short (≤30-turn, ≤16–24k-token) episodes, and LoRA-SFT Gemma 4 on a bf16 dequantization of the exact `-qat-w4a16-ct` checkpoint with error-step masking and a length curriculum, then add one round of rejection-sampling fine-tuning in our own harness. Everything that produced the headline 40–65% SWE-bench Verified numbers for 4B–8B models (full fine-tuning at 80–128k context, 100–340 asynchronous RL updates on B200/H200 nodes, 10k+ frontier-teacher trajectories, Best@8–16 verifier selection) is one to two orders of magnitude beyond ~15 L4x4 hours per week per account, a $50 teacher budget and a 6-minute per-task eval, and the one 4B result at a budget we can approach (SWE-Ladder, ~4,480 rollouts) got **+0.8 pp from GRPO** and **+6.8 pp from privileged self-distillation** ([2610.33667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33667)). The competitive bar is low: the public leaderboard has been capped at **0.24 (14 of ~58 public tasks) since Oct 2** and that score is reached by prompt-only 31B configurations, while the best E4B entry is **~0.06** and one ±1-task swing (0.017) is pure noise ([tracker JSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kawchar85/gemma4-agent-leaderboard/main/docs/data/leaderboard.json), [Utsav README](https://github.com/Utsav-X-bit/Gemma-4-Agent)). A realistic post-training target is 0.26–0.30 public with 31B and a material lift of E4B toward the $10k Small Model Bonus; the paper-track novelty most defensible on our evidence is graph-path-synthesized trajectories plus hop-depth-stratified evaluation on the released code graph, which no published work has done and which the one Gemma 4 localization study explicitly names as open ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958)). The notes disagree in three places that matter (whether to train for compaction, whether E4B findings transfer to 31B, and how much thinking to allow); each is laid out with both positions below.

## Public board caps at 0.24 and one task is noise

The competition is "Google - The Gemma 4 Developer Agent Competition": ~120 hidden Python bug-fix/feature tasks from private repos, PASS/FAIL per task, split public/private, scored sequentially on 4x L4 under a 12-hour wall clock with vLLM TP=4 and `max_model_len` 32,768 ([reconscribe transcription of the rules](https://reconscribe.com/gemma-4-developer-agent-competition-2026/), [fatu/gemma4-dev-agent](https://github.com/fatu/gemma4-dev-agent)). A community tracker polling the leaderboard every five minutes shows **4,081 teams, a maximum of 0.24 held by six teams, median 0.15, mean 0.13, p90 0.18, with 289 teams at or above 0.20** ([kawchar85 tracker](https://github.com/kawchar85/gemma4-agent-leaderboard)). The fifteen distinct score values on the board equal floor(k/58) to two decimals for k = 0..14, and one competitor writes "0.12 (7/58)", so the public split is 58 tasks and one task is worth ~0.017 ([happyc0der/gemma-swe-agent](https://github.com/happyc0der/gemma-swe-agent)). Scoring noise is at least that large: a byte-identical zip scored 0.10 and 0.15 in the same host epoch, and identical files have ranged 0.12–0.22 across evaluator versions ([pilkwangkim Part 2](https://pilkwangkim.github.io/posts/Gemma-4-Developer-Agent-Part-2-Measuring-an-Agent-While-Its-Evaluator-Moved/), [Utsav README](https://github.com/Utsav-X-bit/Gemma-4-Agent)). Any claimed improvement smaller than two tasks on the public board is not evidence of anything; local evaluation on the 129 public tasks (fastapi 67, rich 48, requests 13, httpx 1) with multiple seeds is the only way to see real effects, and even that set is miscalibrated against the scorer until the wheelhouse gaps are fixed (gold patches pass **109/129 naively vs 127/129 calibrated**) ([PR #56 forum digest](https://github.com/emiliodavola/kaggle-gemma-agent/pull/56/files)).

Who is at the top tells us where the headroom is. Every 0.22–0.24 entry documented publicly is prompt and budget engineering on `gemma-4-31b-it-qat-w4a16-ct` with no adapter: the "gemma-4-baseline-lb-0-24" notebook, a "budget-aware coding agent v2" at 0.22, and a "superagent" at 0.18 ([Utsav SOURCES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Utsav-X-bit/Gemma-4-Agent/main/docs/SOURCES.md)). The converged recipe is ~5 min/task, 24–28 tool calls, 240 s command timeout, thinking off or capped at 512–2,048 tokens, temperature above 0.2 with repeat detection in the prompt ([Utsav README](https://github.com/Utsav-X-bit/Gemma-4-Agent), [happyc0der](https://github.com/happyc0der/gemma-swe-agent)). LoRA attempts are few and have not beaten prompt-only: a community rank-16 adapter on the Models tab lists a best public score of 0.18, a first LoRA submission trained on ~1,500 trajectories scored **0.0**, and an alpha/r = 2 adapter induced loops ([PR #56](https://github.com/emiliodavola/kaggle-gemma-agent/pull/56/files)). No public entry uses SWE-smith, SWE-Gym or any RL. The organizers have said distillation from external models is allowed when their licences permit (staff post in thread 742807, as digested), though one secondary guide says no official ruling existed as of Oct 17 ([PR #56](https://github.com/emiliodavola/kaggle-gemma-agent/pull/56/files), [reconscribe](https://reconscribe.com/gemma-4-developer-agent-competition-2026/)).

Realistic targets follow. For the main track, 0.26–0.30 public (15–17/58) would be unprecedented on this board and is the right ambition for a 31B or 12B adapter whose main job is eliminating the loop, non-submission and malformed-call failures that currently cost prompt-only agents tasks. For the Small Model Bonus (highest private-LB E2B/E4B final, $10k, LoRA allowed, still eligible for main prizes and the paper track) the bar is ~0.06, so an E4B adapter that reliably completes and submits could plausibly double or triple that ([discussion 751104 as cited](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/gemma-4-developer-agent/discussion/751104)). The deadlines compress everything: paper track Nov 12 23:59 UTC with a ≤3,000-word Writeup judged on novelty, quality, relevance, verifiability and clarity at 20% each, team merge Nov 25, final Dec 2 ([reconscribe](https://reconscribe.com/gemma-4-developer-agent-competition-2026/)). Our own compute framing (not sourced): 2–3 accounts × ~15 h/week × ~5 weeks is roughly 150–225 L4x4 node-hours in total, including evaluation.

One structural point on E4B versus 31B that colors every later section. The notes agree E4B is the only size where online RL or on-policy distillation is even conceivable on 4x L4 (31B int4 needs all four GPUs for vLLM alone), but they diverge on how much E4B findings transfer. Google's own benchmarks show the agentic gap between sizes is far steeper than the coding gap: **Terminal-Bench Hard 36 (31B) vs 18 (12B) vs 8 (E4B)**, Tau2-telecom 69.3 vs 54.4 vs 18.4, while LiveCodeBench is 80 / 72 / 52 ([Gemma 4 technical report 2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770)). The one controlled graph-tool study found Gemma 4 12B's greedy run degenerate (51% hit the step cap), so 12B tool behaviour is uncharacterized ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958)). Given the 0.24 cap is held entirely by 31B prompt-only entries and 12B still fails to start for some users (`KeyError: vision_embedder.patch_dense.weight` on wheelhouse v30, discussion 748287 as cited in [H](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/gemma-4-developer-agent/discussion/748287)), the sensible split is: E4B as the vehicle for every ablation and for the bonus, 31B for the main-track final, 12B only if its vLLM LoRA path is verified (see the recipe section).

## Free trajectories exist; schema translation is the real data job

The binding constraint on training data is not supply. Tens of thousands of permissively licensed successful trajectories in the OpenHands and SWE-agent schemas are already public: `nebius/SWE-rebench-openhands-trajectories` (**67,074 trajectories, 32,161 successful**, 3,792 issues, 1,823 repos, CC-BY-4.0, native `tool_calls`, average 64 turns) ([Nebius blog](https://nebius.com/blog/posts/openhands-trajectories-with-qwen3-coder-480b)), `nvidia/SWE-Hero-openhands-trajectories` (34,269 trajectories, CC-BY-4.0) ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/SWE-Hero-openhands-trajectories)), `nvidia/Nemotron-SWE-v1` (59k trajectories plus ~210k Agentless-style localization/repair/test JSONL in the Cascade set, CC-BY-4.0) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-SWE-v1)), `SWE-bench/SWE-smith-trajectories` (MIT, with `tool`/`xml`/`ticks` action-format splits) ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench/SWE-smith-trajectories)), `allenai/Sera-4.6-Lite-T2` (25k, Apache-2.0) ([SERA model card](https://huggingface.co/allenai/SERA-8B)), and `Kwai-Klear/SWE-smith-mini_swe_agent_plus-trajectories-66k` (MIT, one bash block per turn) ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Kwai-Klear/SWE-smith-mini_swe_agent_plus-trajectories-66k)). The strongest 8B SFT-only results in the field come from exactly this kind of data: SERA-8B 31.7% Verified from 25k trajectories, Klear 39.0% from 66k, SWE-Lego 42.2% from 18.1k ([2601.20789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20789), [2601.01426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01426)).

What none of them have is our tool schema. The 9 harness tools (`run_command`, `submit_patch`, `get_status`, `read_file`, `edit_file`, `write_file`, `get_code_neighbors`, `search_similar_code`, `get_code_subgraph`) map deterministically from OpenHands (`execute_bash`→`run_command`, `str_replace_editor view/create/str_replace`→`read_file/write_file/edit_file`, `finish`→`submit_patch`), but the three code-graph tools have **zero coverage in any public dataset**, and the harness-specific observation formats (a 150-line read cap, 5,000-character truncation, 300 s command timeouts) differ from every source. This matters more than it sounds: the same SWE-smith-trained 32B scores 32.6–40.2% depending on scaffold, and the same 7B 15.2–17.0% ([SERA table](https://huggingface.co/allenai/SERA-8B), [2602.22124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22124)), and a controlled multi-harness RL study found **harness choice moved results by 7.13 pp, more than the RL gain of under 1 pp** ([2609.04518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04518)). Training on OpenHands-shaped data and evaluating in ADK is the single most likely way to waste the whole GPU budget; the re-mapping must render each trajectory through the competition's exact Gemma 4 chat template with the exact tool declarations, and observations should be re-truncated to the harness's caps.

Scale and shape of the SFT set are dictated by L4 memory and the 6-minute budget rather than by the literature's optimum. Gains appear at ~500 trajectories (SWE-Gym: 7B 1.8→10.6, 32B 7.0→20.6 Verified with 491 trajectories at full FT) and grow log-linearly (SWE-Dev: 7B 13.0% at 574 → 22.8% at 16,639), but below ~2k a 7B stays under ~16% and every 30%+ result used full fine-tuning at 32–128k context ([2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139), [2506.07636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07636)). LoRA evidence is consistently weaker: SWE-Synth's rank-8 LoRA gave +3.3 on 7B Lite and ~0 on 32B at 1k trajectories, and SWE-Protégé reports "LoRA and QLoRA underperformed full SFT" ([2504.14757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14757), [2602.22124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22124)); the counter-evidence that LoRA matches full FT comes from RL, not SFT, where "LoRA matched FullFT's learning even with ranks as low as 1" and rank 32 trailed full FT by 13% after SFT but only 6% after on-policy distillation ([Thinking Machines LoRA](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/), [on-policy distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). The practical target is **2k–5k re-mapped trajectories filtered to ≤30 turns and ≤16–24k tokens**, which SWE-Dev's interaction-budget result supports (34.0 at 30 rounds vs 36.6 at 75) and which keeps us in what the Bud compaction study calls the "8B regime", where short sessions never needed compaction at all ([2506.07636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07636), [2610.25516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25516)). Two cheap data-side tricks are worth +2–4 points at zero extra compute: SWE-Lego's error masking plus trajectory-length curriculum (worth **+2.8 on 8B / +3.8 on 32B**, with a heuristic length curriculum beating a learned difficulty scorer) ([2601.01426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01426)), and Step Rejection Fine-Tuning's finding that keeping critic-judged erroneous steps in context but loss-masked beats discarding failed runs (+3.7 vs +2.4) ([2605.10674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10674)). A LoRA-specific study of trajectory quality on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B found that at 500–1,000 trajectories doubling data cut held-out loss 12.7% while quality selection added under 1%, but at 2,000 quality selection opened a 3.6% gap (p = 0.016) with error-retry rate the dominant sub-dimension, so filter by looping/retry behaviour, not only by pass/fail ([2607.17205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17205)). Reasoning text matters too: SERA found removing reasoning traces dropped 41.0→23.0, so keep the brief rationales in the re-mapped data ([2601.20789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20789)).

For synthetic task generation the evidence favours the cheapest methods. SWE-smith's procedural AST mutation yields **40.2% test-breaking tasks at 0.00 cents per candidate** and trains downstream at 8.6 Verified per 1,000 instances vs 9.2 for PR-mirroring and 8.8 for LM-rewrite, so LM-generated bugs buy almost nothing over free ones ([2504.21798](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21798)). SWE-Synth shows synthetic tasks match or beat real ones at matched trajectory counts (22.2 vs 18.2 at 32B on 2,136 trajectories) and that the re-implementer size barely matters ([2504.14757](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14757)); SWE-Lego reports synthetic tasks give **~66% valid-trajectory yield vs ~27% for real issues** because they are easier ([2601.01426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01426)). Verification of trajectories matters less than assumed: SERA's line-recall thresholds from 0 to 1.0 performed indistinguishably (Kruskal-Wallis p = .066), SWE-Dev's filtering kept 65% of trajectories with nearly identical performance, and SWE-Lego's "semi-resolved" trajectories added +1.2 ([2601.20789](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20789), [2506.07636](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07636)). For us this legitimizes two offline, zero-API sources: procedural mutation and "revert the fix commit" on the 129 public tasks' repos and any pip-installable Python repo with a test suite, and self-generated Gemma 4 31B rollouts kept on a plausibility filter rather than full test execution. The $50 API budget buys roughly 100 frontier trajectories at SWE-smith's ~$0.52 each and should instead go to rationale text for synthesized graph trajectories or to DAgger-style corrections at the hardest ~10% of student states, where a 4B model gained +3.9 (27.3% vs GRPO-alone 11.6%) ([2601.20789](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20789), [2605.12913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12913)). Nothing in the literature measures synthetic-task training on repos absent from pretraining, which is the hidden-test condition; a small held-out set of unseen repos is the only guard.

## SFT and rejection sampling fit; online RL mostly does not

Every published SWE-agent RL result that moved Verified by more than 10 points used 64–128 H100/H200s or 8 B200s and 100–340 updates with 8–10 rollouts per task: DeepSWE took **6 days on 64 H100s** for 23→42.2% ([rLLM docs](https://www.mintlify.com/rllm-org/rllm/examples/deep-swe)), Nebius used 128 H200s and >100 iterations for 11.4→39.0% ([2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03501)), and Arctic-SWE reached 62–65% on Qwen3.5-4B only after **340 asynchronous CISPO updates at 11.6 min each on 8 B200s** ([2610.24914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24914)). First-principles throughput for an E4B policy on L4x4 is ~100–200 trajectories per wall-hour, so one 16-task × 8-rollout update costs about an hour and a SWE-Ladder-sized run (~4,480 rollouts) is 25–40 hours, roughly one to two weeks of the entire team quota; Arctic-SWE's schedule is 300+ hours. These are derived estimates, not measurements, and the first notebook should benchmark them. The outcome of the one 4B run at our scale is sobering: SWE-Ladder's GRPO alone gave 9.6→10.4%, while adding staged privileged on-policy self-distillation (a frozen same-base teacher that sees gold files, F2P tests and the diff per stage, loss = L_GRPO + 0.15 L_OPSD) gave 16.4% ([2610.33667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33667)). The multi-harness study's conclusion that harness mismatch dominates RL gain is the second reason RL must come last: we can only expect any RL signal if training runs inside the exact eval harness.

What does fit is one or two rounds of rejection-sampling fine-tuning. Nebius sampled 10 trajectories per task, kept the 6,548 that passed tests, masked turns with format errors, and ran one SFT epoch (lr 5e-6, batch 64, 50 steps): **11.4% → 20.5% pass@1 from a single round** ([2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2)). SWE-Gym's one round of self-improvement reached 19.7% (32B) and found that sampling compute, not task count, was the bottleneck ([2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139)); SWE-Dev's RFT went 16.2 → 18.0 → 21.2 from 1,000 → 2,000 → 2,300 trajectories ([2506.07636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07636)). With 129 public tasks and a low base pass rate, 8–16 samples per task (1,300–2,000 rollouts) is needed to harvest enough passes, which is why synthetic tasks matter more for RFT than for RL. RFT uses only inference compute, naturally produces data in the exact harness format, and is the cheapest route to an on-policy dataset. Two low-cost refinements ride on it: failure-conditioned recovery data (re-run a failed task with the failed patch and test output in context and keep the recovery; FC-SWE 38.9 → 41.7 R@1 at 4B) ([2610.07898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07898)), and behavioural self-rewarding to choose among same-outcome candidates without new rollouts ([2610.12598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12598)).

The distillation options rank next. On-policy distillation reproduces or beats RL at roughly a tenth of the GPU hours when an open teacher is available (Qwen3-8B: +RL 67.6 AIME at 17,920 GPU-h vs +on-policy distill 74.4 at 1,800 GPU-h) and needs only a teacher forward pass over student samples, so partial rollouts work ([2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1), [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). Gemma 4 31B int4 as the teacher for an E4B student is feasible in principle because all sizes share the 262k Gemini SentencePiece vocabulary ([2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770)), but 31B at TP=4 cannot be co-resident with the student's vLLM and trainer in 96 GB, so the session must be phased (generate student rollouts → unload → score teacher log-probs → unload → train). Privileged self-distillation needs no larger model at all: the 129 tasks' gold diffs and F2P tests are exactly the stage-specific evidence SWE-Ladder uses, and the frozen teacher is the same E4B. Offline step-level DPO is the third option and the one that best uses the $50: Agentic-DPO turns expert trajectories into state-conditioned preferences (expert action vs plausible wrong one-step samples) and took a 9B from 21.7% to 41.4% on tau-bench, "matching online GRPO under the same backbone" with no environment interaction at train time ([2607.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)); the cautionary data point is a 2B search agent where synthetic step-DPO added only ~1 point over trajectory SFT ([2608.29357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29357)).

If a short policy-gradient polish run happens at all (20–40 updates, rank 32, after everything else), the reward design has consensus: gated binary on hidden tests with 0 for malformed tool calls, duplicate calls, no submission or budget exhaustion (Arctic-SWE validates the raw envelope because the parser silently repairs), fraction-of-F2P partial credit with a zero override on any P2P regression (Kimi K2.7, whose one-epoch rank-32 GSPO also shortened trajectories 24–35%), and a step penalty beyond ~10 steps rather than discarding overflow trajectories, because dropping them "removes negative examples of looping behavior" ([2610.24914](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24914v1), [2610.00890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00890), [2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2)). Trajectory-level advantages with sequence-level importance ratios (`importance_sampling_level="sequence"`, `beta=0`, Dr.GRPO scaling, clip-high 0.28) are what TRL exposes and what ARLArena found stabilizing for multi-turn agents ([TRL GRPO docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/grpo_trainer), [2602.21534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21534)). Step-level credit that multiplies rollouts (ARPO, CRR, SWE-MILE, LVRL) is unaffordable, and verl, rLLM, SkyRL and Agent Lightning all assume Ray clusters and Docker-per-task sandboxes that a no-internet Kaggle notebook cannot provide; TRL's `environment_factory`/`rollout_func` with the harness run in-process is the only tooling path, and Unsloth explicitly does not support Gemma 4 RL with vLLM fast inference ([Unsloth Gemma 4 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)). The expected value of the polish run is low single digits; its real purpose would be turn efficiency under the 6-minute budget.

## Looping, not tool choice, is what kills small agents

Small models fail tool use on format adherence and action looping rather than on picking the wrong tool, and the Kaggle record for Gemma 4 matches the literature exactly. A study of small-model function calling found SLMs "struggle significantly with adhering to the given output format" and that fine-tuning beats few-shot beats zero-shot ([2504.19277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19277)). On the competition harness, **28.2% of `edit_file` calls in a 1,140-run corpus arrived missing a required argument** ([dp-pcs/gemma4-tool-call-reliability](https://github.com/dp-pcs/gemma4-tool-call-reliability)); after three identical calls the probability of a fourth was **0.97**, one 55-call loop filled the 32k window, and a missing `<|"|>` delimiter made the parser absorb free text into a file path ([pilkwangkim](https://pilkwangkim.github.io/posts/Gemma-4-Developer-Agent-Part-2-Measuring-an-Agent-While-Its-Evaluator-Moved/)); at temperature 0.2 one entrant saw 23 identical calls and the model sending `submit_patch` to the shell ([happyc0der](https://github.com/happyc0der/gemma-swe-agent)). The one published Gemma 4 SWE-bench Lite run (26B-A4B, 38.67%) found the model "cannot reliably produce OpenAI-style JSON tool calls when there are 10+ tools registered" and that without a step-budget prompt it "burns 100 steps without ever submitting" ([ai-muninn Lite report](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/swe-bench-lite-gemma4-26b-38-percent)). SFT-only small SWE agents in the literature have the same disease: 33.6% of SWE-agent-LM-7B trajectories and 24.4% of the 32B's contain a run of more than 10 repeated actions ([2602.22124](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22124)).

The fixes split into harness-side and weight-side, and the evidence favours doing both in that order. Harness side, the phase-labelled step budget is the best-supported single intervention: the Lite report's step_limit 250 led to never submitting, 30 cut editing short, and **100 with a prompt that split phases (1–15 explore/reproduce, 15–40 locate/edit, 40–60 verify/submit) worked** ([ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/swe-bench-lite-gemma4-26b-38-percent)); a Budget Tracker that tells the agent its remaining budget achieved comparable accuracy at ~10x less budget where simply raising the budget did nothing ([2511.17006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17006)). A forced-submit guard converts non-submissions into partial credit: early in SWE-Master's RL ~20% of trajectories were truncated on budget exhaustion and **24.3% of those would have passed**, fixed by forced submission with a 0.5 reward multiplier plus per-turn "steps remaining" messages ([2602.03411](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03411)); Nebius's "until submitted" retry raised a 72B policy from 16.2% to 22.8% ([2505.13652](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13652)). The harness already grades a non-empty source diff even without `submit_patch`, and scratch files anywhere in `/workspace` leak into the patch, so repro tests belong in `/tmp` ([Utsav README](https://github.com/Utsav-X-bit/Gemma-4-Agent)). A harness-level repeated-action guard is cheaper than training but has mixed evidence: SWE-smith's action resampling on detected repetition cut repetitions but slightly lowered resolve rate to 38.4%, with the authors reading repetition as a symptom of hard or out-of-distribution instances ([2504.21798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798)), whereas SWE-Protégé's loop-penalized RL (hard −10 penalties over only 160 RL steps on 100 tasks) took looping from **31% to 0.8% of trajectories and mean steps from ~60 to ~20** ([2602.22124](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22124)). The practical reading is that a guard should end the loop and redirect (inject a "you repeated this call; the result was X; do something different" message) rather than silently resample.

On format, the schema should be trained in rather than prompted in. With only nine tools, every SFT trajectory can use the exact harness declaration and call format, which is what the function-calling evidence recommends; constrained decoding should at most guard the call envelope, because strict output-format constraints degrade reasoning and the stricter the format the larger the drop ([2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)). Natural-language and programmatic tool calling beat JSON for small open models (+18.4 points, −70% variance; code calling matched or beat native JSON in 11/14 models), but both require changing the harness's tool interface, which is fixed ([2510.14453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14453), [2608.06370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06370)). The cheapest trainable format lever is a brief structured pre-call rationale: at 1.5B on BFCL v3, **0-token CoT scored 44.0%, 32-token CoT 64.0%, 256-token CoT 25.0%**, wrong-function selection fell from 30.5% to 1.5%, and a fixed "Function: [name] / Key args" template matched 32-token CoT and drove function hallucination to 0.0% ([2604.02155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02155)). Error-recovery training is cheap because the harness's failure modes (bad `edit_file` match string, 5,000-character truncation, 300 s timeout, undeclared tool ends the task) are scriptable: PALADIN injected failures into 50k trajectories and LoRA fine-tuning raised recovery rate from 32.8% to 89.7% with 95.2% retained on unseen APIs ([2509.25238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25238)).

The thinking-budget question is where the notes disagree most visibly, and the disagreement is between training-time and inference-time evidence. At inference every Gemma 4 data point says less: thinking "degraded patch quality" with whole-file rewrites in the Lite report and was disabled ([ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/swe-bench-lite-gemma4-26b-38-percent)); with thinking on, 9/16 Kaggle tasks hit the session timeout (6/16 solved vs 13/33 off) ([happyc0der](https://github.com/happyc0der/gemma-swe-agent)); a 512-token thinking cap raised 5→7/22 solved and cut tool calls 57%, and uncapped thinking scored 0.05 vs 0.12 capped ([pilkwangkim](https://pilkwangkim.github.io/posts/Gemma-4-Developer-Agent-Part-2-Measuring-an-Agent-While-Its-Evaluator-Moved/)); "Thinking vs Doing" showed a Gemma 3 12B web agent gains more from scaling interaction steps than thinking tokens ([2506.07976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07976)); and under wall-clock budgets with fast tools, smaller models do better when they can interact more ([2601.16486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16486)). At training time the evidence says keep the reasoning: SERA's 41.0→23.0 drop when reasoning traces were removed, Unsloth's advice to keep ≥75% reasoning examples if preserving thinking, and the Gemma 4 format itself, which for 12B/31B emits an empty thought channel even when thinking is off ([2601.20789](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20789), [Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train), [thinking doc](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/capabilities/thinking)). The reconciliation is to train on short rationales (one to three sentences, well under the harness's `thinking_token_budget` logits processor so the model is never force-closed mid-thought) and to evaluate thinking_budget in {0, 256–512, 1024} on the local set; the harness default of 4,096 is almost certainly wrong for 31B at L4 decode speeds, where 2,000 thinking tokens per step would leave room for only four or five steps in six minutes.

## Graph tools help small models most, but this graph is weak

The code-graph literature gives a clear qualitative answer and the competition's own graph gives a sobering caveat. Across frameworks, a graph plug-in on a fixed repair stage buys **+2 to +6 pp absolute**: RepoGraph lifted Agentless GPT-4o 27.33→29.67 on Lite, OrcaLoca +6.33, ARISE 17.3→22.0 for SWE-agent with Qwen2.5-Coder-32B, SHERLOC +5.95 when locations plus diagnosis were injected ([2410.14684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14684), [2502.00350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00350), [2605.03117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03117), [2606.24820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24820)). The consumption modes that work for non-frontier models are two or three compact tools returning exact entity ids (LocAgent's SearchEntity / TraverseGraph / RetrieveEntity; ToolTrain's five name-based tools), never query languages: CodexGraph's Cypher interface scored **1.95% on Lite with Qwen2-72B vs 22.96% with GPT-4o** ([2408.03910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03910), [2503.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09089)). The one controlled scale study (Qwen3 0.6B–32B plus Gemma 4 E2B/E4B/12B, 79k episodes) finds structured tools help small models more: the gain over grep-only in function Acc@10 peaks at **+17–21 points at 1.7–4B and falls to +4–5 at 32B**, and Gemma 4 E4B goes from 9.6% grep-only to 21.8% with graph tools and 23.7% with graph plus embedding search on a depth-balanced 160-task subset ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958)). The mechanism is specific: E4B's file-level Acc@5 was already 53.8% with grep and did not move; the tools improved function-level precision and exact-id form inside already-found files, which is exactly what `edit_file` needs. Resolution is almost entirely conditional on localization (24–27% vs 1–2%), and oracle locations roughly doubled a weak repair model's solves (13→26 of 160) ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958)).

The caveats are equally specific. Small agents never chain traversals: `find_entity` first reveals gold in 46–53% of hop-0 episodes and `graph_neighbors` in 40–49% of hop-1, but only 12–14% of episodes chain expansions, 64–72% of 2-hop requests were truncated by the 4k-character budget, and each extra hop between issue-mentioned and gold-patched code multiplies the odds of correct localization by 0.544 for E4B ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958)). Checkpoint matters as much as size (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 gained nothing from embedding search while the hybrid Qwen3-4B gained +17.6), forcing tool use did not help, and from 8B upward about half the strict gain is submission form (canonical ids), not discovery ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958)). Untrained small models with graph tools are weak in every study: LocAgent's off-the-shelf Qwen-7B scored func R@5 17.72 vs 34.12 fine-tuned and 62.38 for ToolTrain-7B ([2508.03012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03012)). And the competition's released graph is worse than the research graphs: async functions are absent (host-confirmed), all edges are `calls`, 21.9% of embedding vectors are exact duplicates, `search_similar_code` accepts only symbol names and returns nothing for 57/129 issues, and on hit@5 the embedding tool scores 0.29 and the graph 0.30 against grep 0.41, BM25 0.48 and BM25 over source 0.64 ([aghasalim/gemma4-code-graph-localization](https://github.com/aghasalim/gemma4-code-graph-localization), [HIMANSHUKUMARJHA benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HIMANSHUKUMARJHA/gemma4-graph-retrieval-benchmark)). Dense retrievers lose to agents and to lexical baselines in agentic settings generally; a static 256-d embedder lost 5 of 13 points relative to Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, and LocAgent found embeddings "perform poorly at the function level, even when the patched functions are explicitly mentioned in the query" because they treat the whole issue as the query ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958), [2503.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09089)). The right use of `search_similar_code` is therefore as a hop-0 anchor generator fed short entity-like queries, followed by `get_code_neighbors` along `calls`, which is SpIDER's seed-then-expand pattern (+13–27% relative Recall@20) ([2512.16956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16956)), with grep via `run_command` kept as the primary file-level finder.

Teaching the model the graph tools has three published recipes, none of which has ever been applied to typed-neighbourhood tools on a small model. LocAgent needed only **768 trajectories** (433 Claude plus 335 self-generated, LoRA, 5 epochs, lr 2e-4) to distil into a 7B ([2503.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09089)); ToolTrain's SFT warm-up alone gave most of its gain (file R@5 57.73→79.31 vs 83.11 with RL) and tool-call success rose above 95% during RL even with an outcome-only reward ([2508.03012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03012)); CodeScout trained a 4B by pure RL with an F1 reward against gold-patch functions in 200 steps (1.6k instances × 8 rollouts, 6 turns max) but on 8xH100, and its 1.7B needed an RFT warm start ([2603.17829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17829)). Localization RL is the cheapest RL in the space because it needs string-level rewards, no sandbox and 4–6-turn episodes, but it should run only after SFT shows headroom. The gold patch is increasingly used as privileged evidence rather than as a filter: P2T distilled reference patches into process graphs and gained up to **+10.8 Pass@1** from 1.8k curated instances over outcome-filtered SFT ([2605.21996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21996)), and SWE-Ladder's stage-specific privileged teacher is the same idea in RL form.

This is where the paper-track novelty lies, and it stacks with the main-track plan. No published work synthesizes graph-traversal trajectories from the graph itself: for each of the 129 tasks, compute issue mentions, gold nodes from patch hunks and shortest typed paths in the NetworkX graph, then render them as `search_similar_code` → `get_code_neighbors` → `read_file` → `edit_file` trajectories with brief rationales (teacher only for rationale text, within $50), oversampling hop-2/3 paths to counter the depth gradient. The 2610.33958 preprint explicitly lists "training the agent to chain expansions" as open future work, and P2T and SWE-Ladder never use a code graph. Pair it with a hop-depth-stratified evaluation of the 129 public tasks (Verified's distribution is 37% / 26% / 16% / 15% at hop 0/1/2/3+) so every variant reports per-bucket localization and resolve, which directly uses the released graph dataset the paper track lists under "Graph Reasoning" and turns the community's negative results on these tools into a controlled difficulty axis ([2610.33958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958), [reconscribe](https://reconscribe.com/gemma-4-developer-agent-competition-2026/)). Two trained behaviours extend it: canonical-id discipline (always cite `path.py:Class.func` ids returned by the graph tools, with a harness-side canonicalizer before `edit_file`) recovers the "form" half of the tool gain, and a compact 2-hop closure via `get_code_subgraph` on the top-k neighbours, ranked and deduplicated within the 5,000-character cap, addresses the truncation that kills 64–72% of 2-hop requests. The main risk is that synthesized non-teacher trajectories transfer worse than teacher rollouts, which no prior evidence settles; the checkpoint-specific G/E/T/ET ablation on E4B before any training (about 1–2 GPU-hours of inference) is the control that makes the paper verifiable. A secondary novelty candidate the field also lacks is a specialist-vs-generalist LoRA comparison at equal SWE data: MoRSE's role/subtask adapters beat a rank-matched single LoRA by +3.75–4.0 points at 4B (single seed, SRDD/SciCode not SWE-bench) and subspace contention argues for separate capacity, while compute-matched comparisons find single agents "consistently match or outperform" multi-agent systems except when context utilization degrades, which is precisely the 4B/32k regime ([2608.09251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09251), [2609.03150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03150), [2604.02460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460)). vLLM serves multiple adapters per request for free, so a two-adapter localize/edit split is an affordable ablation, but the generalist adapter must be the baseline.

## Six minutes buys 15–25 steps at 31B; spend them on tests, not thinking

Every published test-time-scaling gain for SWE agents comes from 8–40 full trajectories per task and selection by execution or a learned verifier, at 8–40x token cost: Best@8 captures most of the gain (DeepSWE 42.2% pass@1 → 57.9% Best@8 → 59.0% Best@16), R2E-Gym's hybrid verifier takes 34.4% to 51.0% at Best@26, Kimi-Dev's 40×40 self-play takes 48.0% to 60.4% with 1,600 test runs per issue ([DeepSWE card](https://huggingface.co/agentica-org/DeepSWE-Preview), [2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07164), [2509.23045](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23045)). Even step-level pruning, the method that improves the latency/accuracy frontier rather than the ceiling, took **31.6 minutes per issue on a 4B model** (greedy 18.7 min, parallel 54.1 min) ([2604.14820](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14820)). The wall-clock arithmetic on L4s is unforgiving. The only measured L4 vLLM number is a 7B AWQ model at 50.8 tok/s single-stream and 193 tok/s at four concurrent requests ([vllm-serving-bench](https://github.com/Aman12x/vllm-serving-bench)); scaling by weight bytes (an estimate, not a measurement) puts 31B int4 at TP=4 around 25–45 tok/s, 12B at ~30–35, E4B at ~70–100. At ~30 tok/s and ~400 output tokens per step with thinking off, a step is ~13 s plus tool latency, so **15–25 steps fit in six minutes; at 2,000 thinking tokens per step, four or five do**. Tasks run sequentially on the scorer and a 12-hour overrun currently errors the entire run (staff plan to score unfinished tasks as 0 and advise a moderate `max_time_minutes` failsafe); if public and private are scored in one run of ~120 tasks the budget is ~5.5 min/task, so 5 minutes is the safe ceiling until clarified ([PR #56](https://github.com/emiliodavola/kaggle-gemma-agent/pull/56/files)).

Under that budget the free selection signals come first. Resolved runs finish early (93% of SWE-agent's resolved instances finished under budget vs 69% overall) and experience-driven early termination cut cost 19–55% with at most 0.2% resolve-rate loss ([2405.15793](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15793v2), [2601.05777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05777)), so the phase-labelled budget prompt, a hard `max_tool_calls` around 24–40, and the forced-submit guard should be in the baseline before any model comparison. A regression-test gate before submitting, run via `run_command` on only the modules the diff touches, is the next cheapest: regression tests alone reach 47.4% of R2E-Gym's 51.0% hybrid, SWE-Replay found filtering by regression tests necessary, and Agentless gained +1.3 points from them at $0.01 ([2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07164), [2601.22129](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22129), [2407.01489](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489)). A scratch reproduction test written to `/tmp` before the fix and re-run after is a filter, not an oracle (only 94/300 Agentless repro tests flipped on the gold patch), but reproduction tests gave Agentless its largest selection gain (+5 points) and a 3×3 self-play already beats 40-way majority voting ([2407.01489](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489), [2509.23045](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23045)). The harness strips test and runner-config hunks from the patch, which closes the main reward-hacking vector but also means the repro test must never be the submitted artefact.

Cheap independent review is the one TTS idea with frontier evidence at near-unit cost: a separate critic reviewing the finished diff gave **+3.9 to +6.6 points on SWE-bench Verified at 1.00–1.04x cost** across seven frontier models, while completion gates gave more but "may substantially increase cost" ([2610.34577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34577)). It is implementable as an ADK `agent_tool` on the same base and adapter, with the caveat that it was measured on frontier models and that the one community role-split config on this harness saw skill bypass and verifier starvation ([CharlieHerbst331/gemma4-agent-lab](https://github.com/CharlieHerbst331/gemma4-agent-lab)). Parallel rollouts are conditionally viable: decode at low batch is weight-bandwidth-bound, so measured concurrency 4 gives 3.8x aggregate throughput and N=2–4 rollouts of the same task cost roughly 1.1–1.3x the wall-clock of one if KV memory allows, with selection by regression score, repro-test score, then patch-position agreement as a judge-free tie-break (+3.3 points on gpt-oss) ([vllm-serving-bench](https://github.com/Aman12x/vllm-serving-bench), [2608.22191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22191)). The KV constraint is real: a LoRA adapter shrank KV cache to ~7.6k tokens in early wheelhouses (46k without) before vLLM was changed to size LoRA buffers from the submission, and rank 64 needed `VLLM_MEMORY_PROFILER_ESTIMATE_CUDAGRAPHS=1` to avoid OOM ([discussion 744331 as cited](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/gemma-4-developer-agent/discussion/744331), [Utsav README](https://github.com/Utsav-X-bit/Gemma-4-Agent)). For 31B with ~17 GB of weights, six to eight concurrent 32k sequences is the practical ceiling, so N=2 is the realistic parallelism and should be gated on a measured concurrency test. Replay-from-prefix rather than cold retry when a rollout fails or exhausts budget without submitting is the best use of leftover time: SWE-Replay cut cost 8–17% at equal or better resolve rate, and Restart-Smart with the interrupted diff offered as an overlay took Qwen3.6-27B from 66.6% to 71.8% where a cold restart gave 66.8% ([2601.22129](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22129), [2608.03222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03222)). A trained E4B failure monitor (a 0.6B monitor saved 14.6–20.4% of execution tokens at 5% false positives) is plausible but lowest priority given the deadline ([2608.03222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03222)).

Compaction is the second major disagreement among the notes, and it is partly a factual dispute about the harness. A staff thread reportedly says the evaluator compacts at a `token_threshold` of ~14,336 below the 32,768 ceiling, while one competitor's traces show "ADK never compacts context within a task, so trajectories die at the 32k window after ~20 full-size tool outputs" ([PR #56](https://github.com/emiliodavola/kaggle-gemma-agent/pull/56/files), [happyc0der](https://github.com/happyc0der/gemma-swe-agent)). The literature positions then diverge. Against training for compaction: Compact-Bench finds LLM-summary compaction damages 5–64% of repairs that full-history agents complete and retains ≤16% of recurring details vs 79.1% for mechanical extraction; the Bud study's +12.3-point gain from compaction on a 27B vanished under a 45-minute budget because each summary took a median 161 s, while its 8B submitted after ~10 turns and never needed compaction ([2610.31710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31710), [2610.25516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25516)). On this reading the lever is training the model to finish in 20–30 turns, and any compaction should be mechanical (keep edits, failing-test output, file paths). For compaction-aware training: AutoCompact's SFT-then-RL on compaction decisions gave +9.2 Verified and held at a 16k window, SWE-MeM's trained memory tool is the one 4B result above 40% (43.4%), Context-Folding cut active context ~10x without loss, and ACD's soft-token compression of old observations beat full text under a hard 32k limit (21.1% vs 11.1%) ([2610.02163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02163), [2606.28434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28434), [2510.11967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967), [2609.31430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31430)). The reconciliation that respects both: measure which behaviour the scorer actually has (dump the prompt vLLM receives on a 25-step local run), train primarily for short trajectories, and if compaction does occur at 14k, include trajectories whose prefixes have been replaced by the harness's own mechanical summary so the policy has seen views it did not write; the 150-line read cap and 5,000-character truncation make a Context-Folding-style localizer sub-agent returning a structured "files/functions/suspect lines" summary the one multi-agent design justified on context grounds alone.

## Train on dequantized w4a16 weights, serve unmerged, mind the template

The serving side is fixed: vLLM TP=4, gpu-util 0.80, `max_model_len` 32,768, `gemma4` tool-call and reasoning parsers, PEFT folders with `adapter_config.json` and `adapter_model.safetensors` under `adapters/<name>/`, `max_lora_rank` ≤ 128, submission under 3 GiB, and only the four `-it-qat-w4a16-ct` checkpoints ([reconscribe](https://reconscribe.com/gemma-4-developer-agent-competition-2026/)). All three target checkpoints are `pack-quantized` int4, symmetric, group size 32, Linear-only, activations unquantized, with `lm_head` and the vision/audio towers in the `ignore` list; on disk they are 23.27 GB (31B), 10.26 GB (12B) and 11.51 GB (E4B, where the unquantized per-layer-embedding tables dominate) ([31B config](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it-qat-w4a16-ct/raw/main/config.json), [HF API sizes](https://huggingface.co/api/models/google/gemma-4-31B-it-qat-w4a16-ct?blobs=true)). vLLM never merges a LoRA into the quantized base; it runs the int4 Marlin GEMM plus a separate bf16 LoRA GEMM. That makes the merge-error literature (QA-LoRA, LoTA-QAF) irrelevant, and LoTA-QAF's own finding that "16-bit LoRA achieves superior results" for task-specific fine-tuning supports keeping the adapter unmerged ([2309.14717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14717), [2505.18724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18724)). The only remaining mismatch is the frozen base. A controlled experiment on a Qwen3-4B tool-use LoRA found adapters trained against bf16 vs Q4 weights diverged measurably (cosine 0.9926, **0.146 nats** mean log-prob gap, 3/36 boundary prompts flipped tool choice) while remaining task-equivalent on a narrow eval, and the author's conclusion that training through the deployed quantization and through the bf16 parent "are not numerically the same operation" is the reason to train on exactly the served weights ([HF forum experiment](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/deterministic-lora-training-directly-through-quantized-gguf-serving-weights/179118)). Dequantizing the w4a16 checkpoint to bf16 (int4 × bf16 scale) yields weights bit-identical to what Marlin reconstructs, so train equals serve up to accumulation order; training on `-qat-q4_0-unquantized` is the "0.02–0.15 nats" fallback, and bitsandbytes nf4 QLoRA on top (a different grid, a second quantization error) is the worst option. PEFT cannot wrap the packed modules directly: `get_peft_model` on the compressed model raises `AttributeError: 'Linear' object has no attribute 'weight'`, while loading with `run_compressed=False` dequantized the 31B checkpoint in 217 s and the resulting adapter loaded in vLLM on the w4a16 base with `status: applied` and a measurable effect ([gemma-agent-lab #13](https://github.com/jwj1342/gemma-agent-lab/issues/13)). The documented APIs are `CompressedTensorsConfig(dequantize=True)` in transformers and `convert_checkpoint(..., CompressedTensorsDequantizer)` in llm-compressor; the latter, run once with internet and uploaded as a private Kaggle Dataset, avoids any leftover quantizer state ([transformers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/quantization/compressed_tensors), [llm-compressor convert](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/llm-compressor/en/latest/guides/entrypoints/convert/)).

Memory decides which sizes can be trained at all. Dequantized bf16 weights are 17.9 GB (E4B), 26.7 GB (12B) and 69.9 GB (31B) ([Gemma docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core)); the 262,144-token vocabulary makes unfused logits at 32k ~17 GB at any size, so Liger's `FusedLinearCrossEntropy` or chunked CE is mandatory ([Liger-Kernel](https://github.com/linkedin/Liger-Kernel)). Derived estimates (not measured): E4B text-only with towers dropped and the frozen 5.6 GB PLE table on CPU is ~9 GB of GPU-resident weights and trains DDP×4 at 16–32k, roughly 2,500–4,500 tok/s across four L4s, so a 40M-token epoch (2k trajectories × 20k) is ~3–4 hours; 12B needs FSDP2 sharding (`Gemma4TextDecoderLayer` wrap, Axolotl-verified for Gemma 4) at ~1,200–1,600 tok/s, ~8–10 hours per epoch, one session; 31B is infeasible in bf16 and only feasible with an int4-resident base sharded by FSDP at ≤16k, ~350–550 tok/s, 20–30 hours per epoch, which is the entire weekly quota of two accounts ([Axolotl Gemma 4 notes](https://docs.axolotl.ai/docs/agents/model_architectures.html)). No off-the-shelf exact-match int4-g32 LoRA module exists (bnb nf4 is a different grid, torchao int4 is unsupported by PEFT, HQQ uses its own quantizer), so the 31B path is ~50–100 lines of unvalidated engineering or the demonstrated-but-slow naive `device_map` pipeline (7.6/18.5/18.6/17.6 GiB across four L4s at 4k context with r=16); 4-rank FSDP OOMed host RAM because each rank materialized a ~286 GiB replica ([gemma-agent-lab #13](https://github.com/jwj1342/gemma-agent-lab/issues/13)). This is the compute argument for E4B as the research vehicle and for deciding early whether the 31B final adapter is trained at 16k on the int4-resident path or whether the 31B submission is prompt-engineered with E4B/12B carrying the adapters.

Three model-specific hazards govern target modules. Gemma 4 global layers in 12B/31B use `attention_k_eq_v` (K reused as V; vLLM expects no `v_proj` there) and E4B shares KV across 18 of 42 layers (vLLM drops `k_proj/v_proj/k_norm` at load), so an adapter containing LoRA tensors for those modules will be rejected or silently ignored; restrict `target_modules` by regex to `language_model.layers.\d+.(self_attn|mlp).(q|k|v|o|gate|up|down)_proj`, never `all-linear` or `lora_target_linear: true` (Axolotl reports loss ~12 when vision/audio linears are hit), and diff the saved adapter keys against vLLM's names ([vLLM gemma4 API](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/api/vllm/model_executor/models/gemma4/), [Axolotl](https://docs.axolotl.ai/docs/agents/model_architectures.html)). Do not target `embed_tokens`, `lm_head` (tied) or the PLE tables; vLLM's Gemma 4 class defines no `embedding_modules` and embedding tensors in adapters fail on quantized bases ([vLLM forum](https://discuss.vllm.ai/t/support-for-deploying-4-bit-fine-tuned-model-with-lora-on-vllm/1186)). The 12B's `Gemma4UnifiedForConditionalGeneration` has **no LoRA mark in vLLM's supported-models table**, so 12B must pass a smoke test (`vllm serve ... --enable-lora --max-lora-rank 64 -tp 4` with a zero-init adapter, asserting outputs equal the base) in the competition's exact wheelhouse before any 12B training is scheduled ([vLLM supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models.html)). Known trainer bugs: `use_cache=False` (forced by gradient checkpointing) corrupted Gemma 4 attention in transformers ≤5.5 with loss 10–15 instead of 2–6, fixed by PR #45312 on Apr 9 2026 ([transformers #45242](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/issues/45242)); FlashAttention-2 crashes on head_dim-512 global layers and cuDNN SDPA produced bf16 NaNs on H100, so leave `attn_implementation` unset and run a 50-step NaN check ([prime-rl #2362](https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/prime-rl/issues/2362), [Automodel #3232](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Automodel/issues/3232)); `final_logit_softcapping=30` lives in `text_config` and one trainer silently ignored it, letting logits reach ±122 and doubling grad norms ([prime-rl #2362](https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/prime-rl/issues/2362)); use bf16, never fp16, on L4. Community hyperparameters converge on r=8–32 with alpha ≥ r, lr 1e-4 to 2e-4 (2e-5 for long runs), dropout 0, AdamW, warmup plus cosine, 1 epoch, `max_grad_norm` 0.3–1.0 ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)); the LoRA-without-regret result that optimal LoRA LR is ~10x full-FT LR and nearly rank-independent, with MLP layers mattering more than attention-only, argues for including all seven projections and a rank of 32–64 rather than 128, which also protects KV cache at serve time ([Thinking Machines LoRA](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)).

The format pitfalls are where LoRA submissions have actually died. Gemma 4 turns are `<|turn>role\n...<turn|>` with roles `system|user|model`; `<|think|>` at the top of the system turn enables thinking; reasoning sits in `<|channel>thought\n...<channel|>`; tool schemas are brace-delimited (not JSON), alphabetically keyed, with uppercase types and `<|"|>` as the string delimiter; calls are `<|tool_call>call:NAME{k:<|"|>v<|"|>}<tool_call|>` and results `<|tool_response>response:NAME{...}<tool_response|>` ([prompt-formatting doc](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/prompt-formatting-gemma4), [function-calling doc](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/capabilities/text/function-calling-gemma4)). IT models end turns with `<turn|>`, and fine-tuning "requires adding their respective end tokens" ([2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770)). The official template was revised on Jul 9–15 2026 specifically to suppress `<turn|>` between consecutive assistant messages (linked to repeated tool-call loops), add `preserve_thinking`, open `<|channel>thought` after a tool response when thinking is on, and raise on string-typed arguments ([template diff](https://note.com/unco3/n/ne5d382e3287b?hl=en)); earlier loops were traced to a system prompt rendered as a JSON array and empty `content` on tool-call messages causing a premature `<turn|>` ([OpenCode #21034](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/21034)). Three train/serve consistency rules follow. First, with thinking off the 12B/31B generation prompt ends in `<|turn>model\n<|channel>thought\n<channel|>` (E2B/E4B emit no empty block), so non-thinking training labels must start after that prefix, and after a tool response with thinking off the template appends nothing although the model natively emits the empty block (observed by Dynamo), which must be checked by dumping vLLM's actual prompt ([chat_template.jinja](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it/raw/main/chat_template.jinja), [ai-dynamo PR #17486](https://github.com/ai-dynamo/dynamo/pull/17486)). Second, historical thoughts must be stripped except within a single model turn containing tool calls, where they must be kept; the scorer had a bug where thoughts were dropped between tool calls, since fixed ([prompt-formatting doc](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/prompt-formatting-gemma4), [PR #56](https://github.com/emiliodavola/kaggle-gemma-agent/pull/56/files)). Third, build loss masks yourself (render the prefix with `add_generation_prompt=True` and label only the appended model output plus `<turn|>`, or Unsloth's `train_on_responses_only` with `<|turn>user\n` / `<|turn>model\n` markers) because TRL's `assistant_only_loss` needs `{% generation %}` markers the official template lacks and `chat_template_path` crashes with Gemma 4 processors ([TRL #7775](https://github.com/huggingface/trl/issues/7775), [Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)). The scorer's vLLM 0.19.1 reportedly drops tool calls emitted before the thought channel closes, and the parser's handling of `}`, newlines and literal quotes inside large `edit_file` arguments is unverified, so multi-line code arguments must be round-tripped through the `gemma4` parser before any training data is generated ([vLLM gemma4 parser docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/api/vllm/parser/gemma4/)). The harness's `thinking_budget` is a `ThinkingTokenBudgetLogitsProcessor` with default 4,096 and 0 meaning `enable_thinking:false`; Gemma 4 has no native budget, so training-data thoughts must be short enough never to be force-closed.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the problem. The 40–65% small-model SWE results are not a recipe we can scale down; they are proof that the ceiling is high and that the path to it runs through data shape and harness fidelity first and optimization algorithm last. At our budget the two quantities that actually determine the score are whether the model emits a well-formed, non-repeated tool call every step and whether it submits a diff within the budget; both are properties that 2–5k harness-exact, short, error-masked trajectories plus one on-policy rejection-sampling round can install in a LoRA, and neither requires a single policy-gradient step. The 7-point harness-mismatch penalty that dwarfs RL gains in the literature is, for us, an opportunity: nobody on the leaderboard has trained in the exact ADK format yet, and the one LoRA attempt that scored 0.0 almost certainly failed on template or adapter-key fidelity rather than on data.

The paper and the submission reinforce each other if the graph work is framed as a controlled study rather than a tool advocacy piece. The community has already published the negative result (BM25 beats the released graph and embedding tools), the one academic study says small models gain most from exactly these tools but never chain them, and no one has synthesized traversal trajectories from the graph or stratified evaluation by hop depth. A paper that measures Gemma 4 E4B on all four tool arms before and after graph-path SFT, reports per-hop-bucket localization and resolve, and ships the trajectory generator as a resource is verifiable, novel and relevant on the organizers' own criteria, and its SFT data is the same data that lifts the bonus-track E4B adapter. The open risks worth stating plainly are that no source reports any Gemma 4 size on SWE-bench or any int4-QAT base under LoRA SFT, that E4B tool behaviour may not predict 31B's, and that the scorer's own evaluator has moved under identical submissions; the mitigations are multi-seed local evaluation, measurement before training, and never trusting a one-task difference on the public board.
